# Acton-specific design DNA

This document defines the durable character of the new service-page layer. The global site already supplies identity anchors; this file prevents the new pages from becoming either disconnected redesigns or repetitive SEO templates.

## Inherited site anchors

- Display/headings: Unbounded
- Body and UI: Montserrat
- Primary action color observed on the live site: `rgb(11, 92, 127)` / `#0B5C7F`
- Main visual polarity: black, white, and cool gray with blue-teal actions
- Existing button language: solid blue-teal, white uppercase/bold label, approximately 5 px radius
- Existing subject language: European/luxury service, performance, detailing, protection, real shop work

These are inherited continuity anchors, not the whole service-page design system.

## Core tension

**European precision × New England practicality.**

The site should communicate technical depth without becoming cold, and premium care without becoming a showroom fantasy.

## Brand posture

- Independent specialist, not authorized dealer theater
- Deeply competent, not performatively technical
- Honest advisor, not aggressive repair sales
- Enthusiast-aware, not tuner culture
- Premium through craft and restraint, not black-and-gold luxury shorthand
- Local and real, not a placeless global automotive template

## Visual source world

Start research in the actual subject:

- European service manuals and workshop documentation
- Diagnostic readouts, measurement, alignment, intervals, and calibration
- Metal, rubber, glass, painted panels, fluids, fasteners, and precise tools
- New England weather, seasonal tire logic, salt, cold starts, and local roads
- The independent workshop as a place of judgement and care

Avoid default inspiration from supercar configurators, generic cyber interfaces, and dealer homepage templates.

## Composition

- One dominant visual or idea per view.
- Use open space and controlled asymmetry before boxes.
- Let service evidence carry visual weight: equipment, process, components, and people.
- Use section changes as narrative turns, not alternating bands by habit.
- The European hub may be dense, but density must be organized into readable chapters.

## Typography

- A precise display voice may carry page identity, but body copy must remain calm and highly readable.
- Technical/mono typography is reserved for metadata, intervals, symptoms, measurements, or navigation signals—not entire paragraphs.
- Type roles and rhythm must survive at 390 px without shrinking into brochure text.
- Keep Unbounded and Montserrat unless the assignment is expanded into a global rebrand.

## Color and material

- Extend the existing black/white/blue-teal palette from real workshop/vehicle material.
- No automatic black/red, black/gold, electric blue, or neon-tech solution.
- No gradients on controls. Atmospheric gradients may be used only when the selected direction earns them.
- Contrast must remain measurable; muted copy is still readable copy.

## Imagery

- Prefer real Acton Autowerks photography whenever usable.
- Image roles: place, proof, process, object, atmosphere. Every image must have a job.
- Include people/hands/process where it increases trust; do not rely on empty glamour cars.
- Preserve European/performance appeal without implying manufacturer affiliation.
- Do not fabricate signage, certifications, equipment, technicians, or shop architecture.
- AI imagery, if used for concepting, must be labeled and replaced or approved before handoff.

## UI and interaction

- One strong primary CTA per view.
- FAQ and long brand content use disclosure only when state, focus, and no-JS readability are solved.
- Hover states have focus parity.
- Motion explains hierarchy, orientation, or cause-and-effect. It does not perform the brand by itself.
- Reduced motion shows a complete, stable page.

## Anti-template filter

Reject the direction if it depends on any of the following to feel designed:

- A giant centered headline over a dark car photo
- Endless rounded cards
- Gradient buttons or neon edge glows
- Decorative dashboard-style gauges
- Repeated filled CTAs in every block
- Manufacturer-logo wallpaper
- Random parallax or scroll effects
- Stock phrases and stock images that could belong to any repair shop

## Direction test

Before approval, each visual territory must answer:

1. What specifically makes this Acton Autowerks?
2. Which visual decision could not be reused unchanged for another dealer?
3. What is the single strongest compositional move?
4. How does the design prove competence rather than merely claim it?
5. What survives when motion and imagery are removed?
6. How does the system expand from a short service page to the European hub?
